**Type:** Digital humanities project write-up (summary — full text at the publisher)  
**Year:** 2020–2021  
**Advisor:** Virginia Blanton

Professor Blanton and I had planned an archival trip to the Getty to hunt for
the exemplars behind Royer's 1902 manuscript. The COVID-19 lockdown closed that
door in the summer of 2020, so we rebuilt the project as a computational study:
I transcribed the French text by hand, split it into plain-text files by
manuscript section, and ran the corpus through Voyant Tools.

The result changed how we read the book. The most frequent term is not "Didier"
but "Dieu." A text I had been reading as a life of the saint is closer to a story
about God, with Didier as the supporting character — and "Didier" turns out to
collocate most often with "nom," which pointed us toward the name itself and its
root in the Latin _Desiderius_, "the desired one." Both findings carried into the
[Different Visions article](/academia/different-visions/).
